🔴 Blind Spots & Knowledge Gaps
1
Narrow Investor Base

75% of investors come from the same company (Kanpla). If Kanpla hits trouble, those angels may not be able to follow on. No institutional validation — VC firms haven't touched this yet.

2
No Strategic Investors

No investors from the e-commerce, customer service, or AI infrastructure space. A strategic investor (e.g., Zendesk, Intercom, or an e-commerce platform) could provide both capital and distribution channels.

3
Aggressive Valuation Jump

Valuation went from $2M → $8M (4×) in ~45 days. While ARR grew proportionally ($10K→$85K), this valuation on $85K ARR is ~94× ARR — very high for a pre-seed SaaS.

4
No International Investors

All investors are Danish. For a company targeting global e-commerce, having no international network limits market entry, hiring, and future fundraising rounds.

5
Single Case Study

Only 1 public case study (Viamaja). 85% ticket automation is impressive, but it's a sample size of 1. More diverse case studies across industries would de-risk the investment thesis.

💡 Opportunities to Present
Token Usage Optimization

Wilmo runs AI agents 24/7 on customer tickets. With $35K already spent on "coding agents" pre-funding, their LLM token costs will scale with usage. A 30-60% token optimization could be significant at their target scale.

AI Infrastructure
Multi-Language Expansion

Danish-only investors + Danish-first market. Expanding to English/German/French markets requires localization infrastructure — both for the AI and the sales process.

Internationalization
Investor Network Diversification

Connecting Wilmo to e-commerce focused VCs or angels (e.g., people who invested in Gorgias, Zendesk, Intercom alumni) would fill the "no strategic investor" gap.

Fundraising Strategy
Enterprise Compliance

As they move upmarket (Viamaja is a single case), GDPR compliance, data residency, and enterprise security certifications become blockers. This can be positioned as a service.
